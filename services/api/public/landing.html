Whose money it is

Nothing moves without your approval.

Every payment has to be approved on your own phone, with your face or fingerprint — and that approval covers the exact amount and the exact person being paid. We can pass it on. We cannot write it.

What we can do

Pass on a payment you approved, and pay the cost of sending it.

What we cannot do

Move your balance, change who gets paid, or raise your limits. Not "we promise not to" — the system will not let us.

Changing those rules takes more than one person. Any change to limits or permissions has to be proposed, approved by several people, and then wait out a delay in public before it takes effect. Stopping things is immediate; restarting is the part that has to wait.

Where your money sits

Your euros are held by a regulated e-money institution.

Not by us. The account behind your IBAN is issued and held by Monerium hf., which is authorised and supervised by the Financial Supervisory Authority of the Central Bank of Iceland. Electronic money has to be backed one-for-one and kept apart from the issuer's own funds — that is what the licence is for.

How your money actually moves
  1. Someone pays you. An ordinary bank transfer arrives at your IBAN, the same way a salary would.
  2. Monerium receives and holds it. The euros sit with the regulated institution, backed one-for-one, recorded against your account rather than pooled into a company balance.
  3. It shows up as your balance. Nothing has left yet. You can leave it there as long as you like.
  4. You decide to send some. You choose the country, the amount and the person, and you see the exchange rate and the fee before you commit.
  5. You approve it on your phone. Face or fingerprint. That approval is tied to that exact amount and that exact recipient — it cannot be reused or redirected.
  6. We pass it on and convert it. The euros are exchanged at the rate you were shown, and the money is handed to the payout partner for that country.
  7. It arrives. Into a bank account, a payment app, or as cash at a counter, depending on what you chose.
  8. If any step fails, it comes back. The transfer is unwound and the money returns to your balance, with any costs already deducted listed individually. It does not sit in limbo.
Straight answers

What's live, and what isn't.

Every payments company says “bank-grade” and stops there. We'd rather tell you where the edges are — you'd find them anyway.

Real today
  • Your own IBAN, issued through a licensed European e-money institution, in your name rather than pooled in ours.
  • Approval on your device, enforced by the system itself rather than by our good behaviour.
  • Bank payouts that arrive as an ordinary SEPA transfer.
  • Sign-in with your face or fingerprint. No passwords, nothing to write down.
Not yet
  • No cash has been collected at a counter yet. The cash route works end to end in testing; nobody has walked out with money.
  • We are still in testing, not open to the public, and not holding anyone's real money.
  • Euros only. Dollars and the multi-currency account are designed, not built.
  • We still hold one key used to move money in and out of the bank leg. Handing that to your device is the work in progress.
