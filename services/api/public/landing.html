The account

Is Zold a bank?

No. Zold is an app. Your IBAN and your euros come from Monerium ehf., an electronic money institution licensed in Iceland and supervised by the Central Bank of Iceland. Under the EU E-Money Directive (2009/110/EC), that licence is passported across the European Economic Area, so Monerium can serve you anywhere in the EEA. Zold holds no licence of its own: Monerium looks after the regulated money, and we focus on what we do best, building the software you use it with.

Do I have to leave my bank?

No. Many people start by having their salary or client payments land in Zold. Send money to your other bank any time.

What does it cost?

Opening an account is free, with no monthly fee. Sending euros anywhere in Europe is free.

Do you pay interest?

No. Paying a rate means lending your money out, and we don’t. Your euros stay backed one-for-one, in your name. No rate, no asterisk.

How does the card work?

It spends straight from your euro balance. Use it virtually right away, add it to Apple Pay or Google Pay, or order a physical card.

Can people abroad pay me?

Yes. By euro bank transfer from anywhere in Europe, or in USDC (digital dollars) from outside Europe, through your link.

My employer only takes German IBANs.

In the euro area, an employer can’t refuse an IBAN because of the country it’s from. Your Zold IBAN works for salary like any other.

Safety

How is my money protected?

Your euros are e-money, backed one-for-one and held in an account in your name. We don’t lend them out, which is also why we don’t pay a rate on them. E-money isn’t covered by deposit guarantee schemes.

Who can move my money?

Only you. Each transfer is approved on your phone with your face or fingerprint, for that exact amount and recipient. Zold can’t approve one for you. Two others have a role. If you choose Zoldenburg for recovery, it can move your account to a new phone after checking it’s you, but it can never send money, and your old phone can cancel the move during the wait. Use a backup key of your own for recovery instead, and Zold has no part in it. And Monerium, which issues your euros, can freeze them where the law requires it. The card works differently: Gnosis Pay issues it and it spends from its own card balance, so a tap doesn’t ask for your face or fingerprint.

What if I lose my phone?

With recovery on, you sign in on your new phone, confirm it’s you, and your account moves over after a short wait.

Could a thief empty my account with my phone?

Not with the phone alone. Every transfer needs your face or fingerprint, or your phone’s passcode where the phone falls back to it, so keep a passcode only you know. If your phone is lost or stolen, recovery moves your account to a new phone after a short wait.

What if Zold’s servers were hacked?

Our servers hold no key to your account, so a break-in alone can’t send your money. The app is loaded from our servers, though, so someone who took them over could try to show you one payment and ask you to approve another. Approve only payments you started yourself, and check the amount and recipient each time. What a break-in could expose is the data we keep, such as your email and your payment history.

The company

How does Zold make money?

From paid plans: Premium for people and Business for companies, each with a 30-day free trial. Starter is free. We don’t charge for sending euros, receiving them or converting to and from digital dollars, and we don’t earn anything by lending your money out, because we don’t lend it.

Who is behind Zold?

Zold is made by Zoldenburg. Your IBAN and e-money are provided by Monerium ehf., a licensed e-money institution supervised by the Central Bank of Iceland.
