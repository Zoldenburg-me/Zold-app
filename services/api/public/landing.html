Whose money it is

Your device signs. We only submit.

Before a cent moves, the vault demands a signature from the key on your phone — over the exact amount, and over exactly who is being paid. We can carry that signature to the chain and pay the gas for it. That's the whole of our authority.

What we can do

Submit a payment you signed, and cover its gas.

What we cannot do

Move your balance, redirect a payout, or raise your limits on our own. The contract refuses us — not our policy.

Changing the rules takes more than one key, too. The contracts answer to a multi-signature timelock, so any change to limits or roles is queued in public before it can take effect. Stopping the system is instant; starting it again is the part that has to wait.

Straight answers

What's live, and what isn't.

Every payments company says “bank-grade” and stops there. We'd rather tell you where the edges are — you'd find them anyway.

Real today
  • Your own IBAN, issued through a licensed EU e-money institution and attached to your account, not ours.
  • Device-signed payments, enforced by the contract rather than by our server's good behaviour.
  • The MoneyGram protocol, running live against a public Stellar anchor.
  • Bank payouts that burn e-money and send a real SEPA transfer.
  • Passkey sign-in, verified end to end. No passwords, no seed phrase.
Not yet
  • No cash has actually been collected yet. The corridor is protocol-complete and unproven with real funds.
  • Settlement runs on a test network while the public deployment is prepared.
  • Euros only. Dollars and the multi-currency account are designed, not built.
  • We still hold one signing key for the e-money leg. Moving it to your passkey is the open work.

If that second column looks unusually candid for a landing page — it's the same list we keep internally.
